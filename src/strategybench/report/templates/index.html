How exploitable is each AI player?

StrategyBench asks a subject to make every decision in a small poker game many times, estimates its strategy from the answers, and computes the exact best response to that strategy with game-tree search. Exploitability is how much that best response wins per hand beyond the equilibrium value, averaged over both seats. 0 means unexploitable.

Results describe a model together with its prompt and sampling settings, on small games. They are not a measure of general poker or reasoning skill. See the methodology.

How to read this table
  • Exploitability (M1) is the headline: what a best-responding opponent gains per hand against the subject's estimated strategy, beyond the equilibrium value, averaged over the two seats. Values are in {{ game.rows[0].unit }}s per hand. The interval is a 95% bootstrap interval over the subject's samples.
  • Bias-corrected: a maximum over opponent strategies turns sampling noise into apparent exploitability, so the plain estimate is biased upwards at small N. The corrected value subtracts the bootstrap estimate of that bias; larger N is the reliable remedy.
  • Best-response gain (M2) splits M1 by the seat the subject sits in (M1 is their mean).
  • Loss vs equilibrium (M3) is what an opponent playing the equilibrium, without adapting, wins from the subject. It is always at most M2, and can be near 0 even when M1 is large.
  • Parse failures and coverage are reliability measures (M4). Failed replies are retried and counted, never replaced with a guessed action.
