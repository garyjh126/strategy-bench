How exploitable is each AI player?

StrategyBench asks a subject to make every decision in a small card or dice game many times, estimates its strategy from the answers, and computes the exact best response to that strategy with game-tree search. Exploitability is how much that best response wins per {{ episodes }} beyond the equilibrium value, averaged over both seats. 0 means unexploitable.

Results describe a model together with its prompt and sampling settings, on small games. They are not a measure of general game-playing or reasoning skill. See the methodology.

How to read this table
  • Exploitability (M1) is the headline: what a best-responding opponent gains per {{ game.episode }} against the subject's strategy, beyond the equilibrium value, averaged over the two seats. Values are in {{ game.rows[0].unit }}s per {{ game.episode }}.
  • A best responder exploits sampling noise too, so the exploitability of the estimated strategy (plug-in) is biased upwards, most of all near equilibrium. For sampled subjects the table shows the bias-corrected estimate with a 95% basic bootstrap interval, which is centred on it. These intervals are approximate: in simulation they cover the true value less often than 95% in Leduc, and much less often for subjects very close to equilibrium (see the methodology). Larger N is the reliable remedy. Exact subjects (probabilities read directly) have no sampling error.
  • Best-response gain (M2) splits M1 by the seat the subject sits in (M1 is their mean).
  • Loss vs equilibrium (M3) is what an opponent playing the equilibrium, without adapting, wins from the subject. It is always at most M2, and can be near 0 even when M1 is large.
  • Parse failures and coverage are reliability measures (M4). Failed replies are retried and counted, never replaced with a guessed action.
